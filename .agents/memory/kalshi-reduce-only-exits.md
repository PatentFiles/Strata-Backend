---
name: Kalshi reduce-only exit semantics
description: Why closing a funded YES or NO position needs V2 reduce-only plus non-resting execution
---

Kalshi's V2 event-order contract quotes both directions in YES dollars: closing owned YES is an ask at the YES bid; closing owned NO is a bid at one minus the NO bid. `reduce_only` is documented as capping order **placement** quantity by the member's current position, not as a promise that a resting order will continuously shrink when external account inventory changes. Use immediate-or-cancel for automated exits unless execution-time enforcement is independently established. For IOC, `remaining_count` describes the final resting remainder after unfilled quantity is canceled; it is not the original unfilled quantity. Primary-subaccount position reads must be matched to primary-subaccount order and fill reads.

**Why:** An ordinary legacy sell can open the opposite exposure; a reduce-only GTC order could become unsafe if a separate transaction changes holdings after placement. A raced cancellation's `reduced_by` can be smaller than an earlier remaining count because of a fill; only subsequent order, fill, and net-position evidence proves the outcome.

**How to apply:** Never fall back to ordinary sells on a V2 failure. A partial IOC can have one fill and zero remaining after canceling unfilled contracts: do not enforce filled plus remaining equals requested on an IOC. Attribute execution only through authenticated order identity, complete fills and fees, balance, and net-position evidence, then keep partial exits frozen and one-use. Treat a rejected, uncertain, or quantity-capped V2 exit as frozen and reconcile authenticated evidence. Keep the live-entry fee guard fail-closed until an authoritative current fee ceiling includes entry fee rounding; do not substitute a future exit-fee forecast for the holding-to-settlement entry risk calculation. A standing fee-policy authorization never substitutes for fresh market, series, event, and fee-change reads at each submission.

Kalshi allows 0.01-contract fills. Its documented balance rounding floors the combined signed trade revenue **minus trade fee** to the member's balance precision, with order-level rounding accumulation and bounded rebates. Direct-member balances align to $0.0001 (centicent); non-direct balances align to $0.01. For a non-direct account, a buy can debit a full cent for *each* 0.01-contract fill even when the model trading fee for a whole contract appears tiny. A safe entry bound must permit up to 100 such independently rounded pieces per whole contract; using a whole-contract fee alone is not a guaranteed ceiling.

**Why:** At a 40-cent limit, 100 separate hundredth-contract fills could each debit one cent; a single whole contract can therefore exhaust a $1 cash-loss cap. Official series/event fee metadata names the fee model and multiplier, but does not by itself prove the applicable standard coefficient or exclude every product exception on a paginated public fee schedule.

**How to apply:** Bound *total possible buy debit* for the smallest allowed fill partition, including balance precision, and account for actual fees already paid **plus** unfilled quantities separately. In the approval UI, label the one-cent per-small-fill assumption as a deliberately conservative safety bound, separate from any PDF centicent rounding rule. The canonical official PDF has returned HTTP 429 to direct GETs from this workspace; a bounded read-only monitor and per-submission verification must treat that as unavailable and block execution, never substitute a cached mirror or silently trust old bytes. The administrator-reviewed official PDF authorizes a versioned, persisted standing policy only if the live canonical bytes and market applicability are independently verified; its temporary review may expire, but the approved policy does not expire with time. Every entry and exit must still fail closed unless fresh applicable fee evidence and exact total loss bounds are verified. Current public metadata cannot independently prove the PDF's global standard coefficient has not changed or applies to every market, so do not claim uninterrupted automated operation is fully cleared on that basis alone. A new approval must not reset cumulative historical debit or fee exposure. Exit fills and position reconciliation remain separate.
