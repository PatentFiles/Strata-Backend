description: Live API edge cases for conservative client-order recovery and fee accounting.

Do not require every exchange order to have a nonempty client-order ID. An unrelated live account order had an empty string; it is non-matching evidence, not a reason to discard the entire order-history scan. A historical-fills GET returned 404 for a synthetic ticker even while order-history GET worked; 404 must not mean “no fills.” If historical fills are unavailable, require the current fills for the candidate order to account for its reported total fill count and be visible to normal startup reconciliation, or keep the reservation blocked.

**Why:** Read-only signed GETs against the configured account exposed response cases absent from initial mocks. An earlier strict parser discarded usable order evidence; treating an unavailable history as zero fills would be unsafe. A canceled partial order can hide aged-out fills when history is unavailable; even a zero net account position can mask offsetting missing buys and sells. Always compare the exchange's authoritative filled count with the deduplicated fill sum before startup readiness. Settlement is account-market-wide, so a complete *current* fill snapshot cannot rule out unrelated aged-out activity or even a zero-fill manual order on the same market.

**How to apply:** Model unrelated empty client IDs as null, match only an exact nonempty reserved ID, and cross-check exchange order totals against fill records, including canceled orders. Do not infer rejection from an empty resting-order list. Order recovery may use current fills despite historical 404 only when authenticated order totals prove all fills; **settlement attribution is stricter** and requires complete current and historical ticker-scoped orders/fills to prove exclusive market ownership. Any unrelated order, even unfilled, or incomplete history blocks it. A GET-only audit with frozen execution gates must never grant startup execution readiness or imply full historical verification; a matching current snapshot proves only what was observed, because aged-out unrelated activity may be missing. Kalshi's net per-fill fee already incorporates order-level rounding rebates; do not add order-level fees again. Keep fixed-point dollars until total P&L is calculated. The two BTC records previously called payout mismatches are explained by **account-wide YES/NO netting**, not erroneous exchange payouts. A NO sell order's fill can be expressed as a complementary YES sell, and settlement gross side counts can include this conversion. Across 71 read-only market records, settlement `fee_cost` exactly equaled the sum of per-fill fees, so it is cumulative account-market fee evidence, **not another settlement debit**. Never allocate an account-wide mixed-side record to a sole local strategy position; prove local ownership and otherwise block.
